From the last hidden state to logits

p. 49
What the book says

The final layer multiplies the last hidden state by the unembedding matrix, which gives one number per token in the vocabulary: the logits. A 128k-token vocabulary means 128k logits for every generated token.

What it means

Think of the logits as raw scores, one per possible next token. They are not probabilities yet: they can be negative, and they do not add up to anything in particular. For the prompt “The cat sat on the”, “mat” might get 7.1, “floor” 5.3 and “banana” −2.0.

Softmax turns logits into probabilities

p. 50
What the book says

Softmax exponentiates each logit and divides by the sum, so the outputs are positive and add up to one. The book's example uses three logits and shows the top token taking most of the probability mass.

What it means

SoftmaxSoftmax learning · 60% Turns a vector of scores into probabilities that sum to 1.Also inUnderstanding Deep Learning, p. 72 (lesson “Loss functions · Softmax”) Leads toScaled dot-product attention · Cross-entropyOpen that lesson does two things at once: e^{z} makes every score positive, and dividing by the total makes them add up to 1.

p_i = \frac{e^{z_i / T}}{\sum_j e^{z_j / T}}

Move the temperature: at T<1 the top token takes almost everything; at T>1 the rare tokens get a chance.

Temperature, top-k and top-p

pp. 50–51
What the book says

Sampling settings change which token is picked: top-k keeps the k most likely tokens, top-p keeps the smallest set whose probabilities add up to p, and temperature reshapes the distribution first.
