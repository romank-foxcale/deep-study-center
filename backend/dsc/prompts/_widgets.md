Fields: `expr` in x and y with the plot syntax, e.g. "0.1*x^2 + 2*y^2" or "(1-x)^2 + 5*(y-x^2)^2"; `xmin`, `xmax`, `ymin`, `ymax`; `start` [x, y]; `optimizers`: a subset of "sgd", "momentum", "rmsprop", "adam"; `lr` (a learning rate that shows the point, e.g. one where plain descent zigzags); `steps` (20-150)), formula (an explorable formula: the key formula of a section with each important part in its own color; hovering a part and the legend under the formula say what it means; each parameter has a slider; the formula is shown again with the learner's values and its result, a line explains what a change did, and a plot follows the change). Keep numbers within -10..10 and add a one-line caption.
For a formula: `tex` is the TeX with {{id}} for each part, e.g. "p(x) = \frac{1}{{{norm}}} \exp\left(-\frac{(x - {{mu}})^2}{2{{sigma}}^2}\right)"; `parts` (2-4) each have `id`, `tex` (may contain other {{id}}, e.g. norm = "{{sigma}}\sqrt{2\pi}"), `label` (at most 8 words: what the part does, e.g. "mean: where the bell is centered"), `color` (green, purple, pink, orange, blue or teal; one per part) and, for a parameter, `param` (its name in `formulaParams`). `formulaParams` (1-3): name of lowercase letters (x too: then the learner sets x, and a plot over x marks it; `resultExpr` may use only parameters), min, max, value, and `effect` (one short sentence: what happens when it grows, e.g. "larger σ: the bell gets wider and lower"). Use at most one formula widget in a lesson, for its central formula (the learner can make a separate formula lesson for any other formula, and that lesson uses the widget fully); label only the parts the learner has to understand, and do not label both a part and a part inside it.
"What if" (explains WHY the formula is as it is; the learner clicks a part and types another form for it, and sees the real formula and theirs side by side, with both curves and the checks): give every part an `expr` (the whole part in the same calculator syntax, e.g. "x^2" for x², norm = "sigma*sqrt(2*pi)"; the learner edits the whole colored part), write `plotExpr`/`resultExpr` with [id] for every part, and give each part whose form is a choice (a constant, a root, a power, a sign, a normalizer) 2-4 `variants`: plausible wrong forms, each with `tex`, `expr` and `why` (one or two sentences: what breaks with this form, and so why the real one is right, e.g. for "sigma*sqrt(3*pi)": "the area under the curve is no longer 1, so the curve is not a probability distribution"). Then write `plotExpr`/`resultExpr` with [id] in place of that part, e.g. "exp(-((x-mu)^2)/(2*sigma^2))/[norm]", so a variant changes the plot and the result. Add `checks` (1-3) that show what the real formula makes true: kind "area" (area under the plot over its range), "peak" (its highest value), "at" (its value at `at`) or "result" (the result), each with a short `label` and, when the real formula must give a known number, `expect` (e.g. area, expect 1). A variant's `why` must agree with what the checks will show.
